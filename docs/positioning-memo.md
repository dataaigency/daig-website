# Positioning memo: data aigency in the 2026 consultancy landscape

**Prepared by:** commercial specialist agent · **Date:** 2026-08-20 · **Status:** proposal, nothing implemented

Scope: a small market study of how data and AI consultancies pitch themselves in 2026, then a positioning proposal for data aigency. Research only. No site files were changed. Owner-dictated strings (hero title, the three problems items, the intake-call CTA framing, the eyebrow) are treated as immovable and nothing below touches them.

---

## a) Landscape summary

1. **Everyone opens with the same transformation sentence.** Snowpack Data says "Transform your data into real business value", Tasman says "Transform your complex data into confident decisions", Aimpoint Digital "builds enterprise AI and modern data solutions". Same verb, same abstraction, interchangeable brands. Sources: [Snowpack Data](https://snowpack-data.com/), [Tasman](https://tasman.ai/), [Aimpoint Digital](https://aimpointdigital.com/).

2. **The sameness is measured, not just felt.** In Wynter's survey of 100 B2B SaaS marketing leaders, 94% admitted their messaging barely stands out. A Gartner survey of 1,100+ B2B buyers found 64% could not tell one brand's digital experience from another's. Sources: [Wynter](https://wynter.com/post/b2b-brand-differentiation), [Brandingmag on the Gartner finding](https://www.brandingmag.com/kevin-perlmutter/get-real-to-differentiate-your-b2b-brand/).

3. **Proof follows firm size.** Big and mid-size shops show client logos and hard metrics (Thoughtworks: "66% faster time-to-market", Bosch and Bayer case studies; Aimpoint: "98% reduction in response time", Databricks Partner of the Year). Buyers' guides tell prospects to check exactly this: vendor partner tier and count of certified engineers. Sources: [Thoughtworks data practice](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/what-we-do/data-modernization), [Aimpoint Digital](https://aimpointdigital.com/), [dataconsultingfirms.com](https://dataconsultingfirms.com/).

4. **Boutiques pitch against the Big 4 on speed, seniority and price.** The recurring claims: ship 2 to 5x cheaper, senior people do the work, "the people you talked to stay on your project". This is now itself a crowded message. Sources: [Justin McKelvey's vetting guide](https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/ai-consultant-companies), [Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/ai-consulting-for-small-business), [Intellectyx](https://www.intellectyx.com/boutique-ai-consulting-firms/).

5. **"AI-ready data" is the new consensus headline.** After MIT's 2025 finding that ~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed no ROI, nearly every data shop repositioned around it. Thoughtworks leads with "AI is only as smart as the data you feed it". The failure causes named in the MIT coverage are integration and governance, not model quality. Sources: [Forbes on the MIT report](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andreahill/2025/08/21/why-95-of-ai-pilots-fail-and-what-business-leaders-should-do-instead/), [complexdiscovery summary](https://complexdiscovery.com/why-95-of-corporate-ai-projects-fail-lessons-from-mits-2025-study/), [Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/what-we-do/data-modernization).

6. **Fractional data leaders sell headcount math, not craft.** The pitch is "senior expertise without the full-time hire", 1 to 2 days a week, strategy plus some hands-on. Deliverables and handover are vague. Sources: [Go Fractional](https://www.gofractional.com/hire/head-of-data), [Dub Dub Data](https://www.dubdubdata.com/Fractional-data-leadership-ai-consulting), [Snowpack fractional page](https://snowpack-data.com/services/fractional-data-leadership).

7. **Handover and ownership exist as a message, but almost never lead.** Tasman promises knowledge transfer and no vendor lock-in; a buyers' guide notes strong boutiques "leave behind a system your team can maintain". Yet in every case it sits mid-page, behind the transformation headline. Nobody makes leaving the whole pitch. Sources: [Tasman](https://tasman.ai/), [HelpWithMetrics](https://helpwithmetrics.com/blog/top-companies-for-data-analytics/).

8. **Solo practices are told to compensate with personality and thought leadership.** The standard advice: personal authority, a face, testimonials, frictionless booking. Almost none of the advice says "show the actual work". Sources: [Melisa Liberman](https://www.melisaliberman.com/blog/consulting-website-examples), [Freelance Cake](https://www.freelancecake.com/blog/consultant-website-examples).

9. **Nobody shows the architecture.** Across every site reviewed, the work itself is invisible: text, logos, stock metaphors. Not one competitor puts a readable diagram of what they actually build on the homepage. The closest thing to "proof of thinking" is blog posts. This is the emptiest space in the whole landscape.

10. **Everyone writes for data leaders only.** Copy across the field assumes the reader knows what a semantic layer, data mesh or medallion architecture is. Founders, ops leads and domain managers who increasingly sit in the buying committee are left out of the conversation.

## b) The three gaps data aigency can own

### Gap 1: Show the work. The site is the proof.

Every competitor claims outcomes; almost none show what they build. A solo practice cannot out-logo Thoughtworks or out-metric Aimpoint, and inventing numbers is off the table. But data aigency already has something no competitor shows: readable, animated architecture diagrams for every service, with plain captions. That is proof of the two things a buyer actually worries about with a consultant: can this person explain clearly, and will I understand what I am paying for. The move is to name this explicitly. Not "we deliver outcomes" but "here is the blueprint, read it yourself". The diagrams stop being decoration and become the evidence. No other firm in the study does this, and a big firm structurally cannot: their delivery varies by team, so they cannot promise one legible design.

### Gap 2: Built to leave. Handover as the lead promise, not the footnote.

Lock-in is the quiet fear in every consulting purchase, and for a solo practice the fear doubles: what if this one person disappears? Competitors answer it defensively, in paragraph four. data aigency can invert it: the bus-factor objection becomes the pitch. Everything lives in your repositories from the first commit, open table formats, documented decisions, runbooks and keys at the end. "Nothing depends on me staying" is a sentence no ten-person shop can say as credibly, because their business model is the retainer. The site already carries this in the about page and the process line; the gap is that no competitor leads with it, so data aigency can, and it converts the practice's biggest structural weakness into its most distinctive claim.

### Gap 3: Governed AI, explained past the CTO.

Post-MIT-95%, "AI-ready data" is what everyone says, in language only a head of data can parse. Meanwhile the MIT coverage itself says failures come from adoption and governance, not models. The open position is to be the firm that explains governed AI adoption in plain words to the whole buying committee: one use case at a time, measured before it scales, on a rail of access control and human sign-off. The governance flow diagram on the site already draws exactly this. Owning the gap means keeping every sentence readable by a founder or an operations lead, not just a CTO. That is both a values choice (humane, inclusive) and a commercial one: the person who forwards the link internally is rarely the most technical person in the room.

---

## c) Recommended positioning statement

> **One architect who builds your data platform in plain sight, explains it in plain language, and hands it over completely. AI governed, never hyped.**

(24 words. "Plain sight" is gap 1, "plain language" is gap 3, "hands it over completely" is gap 2.)

## d) Copy proposals (before / after)

All strings live in `src/locales/en/common.json`. Immovable strings are untouched. Nothing below is implemented; these are proposals.

### 1. About intro (`about.title`, `about.body1`)

**Before**
> One architect, end to end.
>
> data aigency is the remote practice of Vadim Lucas. The person who takes your first call is the person who designs the architecture, builds it and hands it over. Nothing is lost between a salesperson and a delivery team.

**After**
> One architect, end to end.
>
> data aigency is the practice of Vadim Lucas. The person who takes your first call is the person who designs the architecture, builds it and hands it over. No salesperson, no junior team behind the curtain. Every diagram on this site is drawn the way your documentation will be written: so anyone on your team can read it.

Rationale: keeps the title, keeps the anti-handoff point, and adds the gap 1 claim in one sentence. Drops "remote" quietly (no location claims either way, and "remote" invites a location question). The last sentence turns the site's diagrams into stated proof.

### 2. Services page intro (`services.label`, `services.title`, new key `services.sub`)

**Before**
> What we build
> Four things, done properly.
> (no sub line)

**After**
> What we build
> Four things, done properly.
> Each one comes with a diagram you can read without a data background. What you see drawn here is what gets built, tested, documented and handed over.

Rationale: label and title already work; the missing piece is a sub line that tells the visitor the diagrams are the promise, not decoration. Sets up gaps 1 and 3 in two calm sentences. New key `services.sub`, rendered under the title on both the homepage services block and the services page (component keeps using t() keys).

### 3. Dictionary definitions (`dictionary.entries`)

**Entry 1, before**
> The capacity to act. You regain agency over your data: one source of truth, owned by you and documented for you.

**Entry 1, after**
> The capacity to act. Your data in one trusted place, owned by you and documented for you, so decisions stop waiting on it.

Rationale: "regain agency over your data: one source of truth" restates the word being defined. The rewrite ends on what the reader gets (decisions stop waiting), which is legible to a non-technical reader.

**Entry 2, before**
> A consulting practice. We design the architecture, build the pipelines and hand everything over. Scope, timeline and costs are agreed before we start.

**Entry 2, after**
> A consulting practice of one architect. Design, build, then a full handover: code, docs, runbooks and keys. Scope, timeline and costs agreed before we start.

Rationale: names the solo shape as a feature and makes the handover concrete (gap 2). "Keys" echoes the process section, so the site repeats one idea instead of three.

**Entry 3, before**
> Agency with AI built in. Adopted in phases, secured and governed from the start. Not a vibe-coded data strategy.

**Entry 3, after**
> Agency with AI built in. One use case at a time, measured before it scales, governed from day one. Not a vibe-coded data strategy.

Rationale: "adopted in phases, secured" is abstract; "one use case at a time, measured before it scales" is the concrete version and matches the governance diagram word for word (gap 3). Keeps the closing line, which is the site's best sentence.

### 4. New homepage section proposal: "What handover looks like" (spec only, do not build yet)

The study justifies one new section. The two strongest proof formats in the field are client logos and metrics, and both are closed to us until real cases exist. The open format is the sample deliverable: show the visitor exactly what they hold at the end, before they ever book a call. No competitor reviewed does this.

- **Placement:** between the process section ("No black boxes") and the about section. It is the evidence for the process claim made directly above it.
- **Content:** one navy statement section titled "What you keep." Three artifacts shown as visual cards, drawn in the site's diagram style, not screenshots: (1) a page of an architecture document with visible headings and a small diagram, (2) a pipeline test run with green passes and one red fail routed to "alert, engineer first", (3) a runbook page plus a repo tree labeled "your repositories". Each card gets a one-line caption in plain language.
- **Honesty rule:** every artifact is clearly labeled as a sample ("sample page from an architecture doc"). No fictional client names, no invented metrics, nothing styled to look like a real engagement.
- **Copy kit (new i18n keys under `handover.*`):** label "The deliverables", title "What you keep.", sub "Every engagement ends with a handover, not a renewal conversation. This is what lands in your hands.", captions per card, no CTA of its own (the contact section follows soon after; one primary action per page).
- **Motion:** cards reveal on scroll like the existing flow diagrams; fully static under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Depends on:** proof asset 1 below. Build the sample doc first, then the section.

## e) Proof assets to build next, in order

1. **A sample architecture document (sanitized template, ~4 to 6 pages).** Feeds the new homepage section and can be handed out after intake calls. Highest leverage because it proves gap 1 and gap 2 at once and requires no client permission. Label it as a sample everywhere.
2. **The first real case study for /work.** Already an open item in the PRD. Real engagement, real numbers only where they are real, "before and after" drawn in the site's diagram style. Until one exists, publish build-log style working notes on /work so the section is not empty.
3. **A public starter repo on GitHub.** A small, well-documented lakehouse or dbt project template under the data aigency account. Buyers' guides tell prospects to check certified engineers and partner tiers; a solo practice's equivalent verifiable signal is public code with tests and docs. Link it from the footer GitHub icon so the icon lands somewhere that sells.
4. **A sample intake-call summary (one page).** The contact page already promises "a short written summary, whether we work together or not." Showing a sample of it raises the perceived value of the free call without changing its framing.
5. **Real certifications, if held.** Fabric or GCP credentials listed plainly on the about page. Only if genuinely held; never padded.
6. **A 3 to 5 minute narrated walkthrough of one diagram.** Later, once 1 to 3 exist. A calm voice explaining the medallion flow doubles as proof of the "explains it in plain language" claim and gives LinkedIn material.
