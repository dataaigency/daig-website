"Just use an EU model" is the kind of sentence that sounds like an answer
and isn't. It skips the actual question, which is: EU-owned, or just
EU-hosted, and does that distinction even matter for what you're building?
Both halves of that question got sharper this month, so here is the honest,
current map.

## What actually changed this month?

Two things. First, enforcement for general-purpose AI models has been live
since 2 August 2026: the Commission can now request a provider's technical
documentation, get access to a model for evaluation, and require corrective
measures, including recalls
([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place)).
Signing the EU's General-Purpose AI Code of Practice is voluntary, but it
buys a documented compliance path and counts as a mitigating factor if a
provider ever gets fined
([artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/code-of-practice-overview/)).
That makes "did our vendor sign it" a real due-diligence question now, not a
nice-to-have.

Second, Mistral, the closest thing Europe has to a flagship LLM provider,
shipped real product news in September: OCR 4.1 went generally available at
€3.50 per 1,000 pages with bounding boxes and confidence scores, alongside
a new Agentic Search retrieval layer and more than 20 connectors including
Google Drive and GitHub
([getaibook.com](https://getaibook.com/news/bounding-boxes-arrive-in-mistral-ocr-4-for-agentic-retrieval/)).
That builds on the $830 million in debt financing Mistral raised in March
from a consortium including Bpifrance, BNP Paribas and Crédit Agricole, to
buy 13,800 Nvidia GPUs and 44MW of power for a data centre near Paris, with
a stated goal of 200MW across Europe by the end of 2027
([DataCenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/mistral-ai-raises-830m-in-debt-financing-for-data-center-in-paris-france/),
[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/30/mistral-ai-paris-data-center-cluster-debt-financing.html)).
The infrastructure and the product roadmap are catching up to the marketing
at the same time enforcement teeth arrive. Worth a fresh look.

## Is "EU-hosted" one thing?

No, and collapsing it into one thing is the mistake I see most often. It
forks three ways, and the fork runs on two separate questions: who legally
owns the company behind the model, and whether an EU-region host actually
exists for the specific model version you plan to call.

I wrote the residency-versus-sovereignty distinction up properly in
[an earlier post](/work/2026-09-eu-region-not-sovereignty): an EU data
centre answers where the bytes sit, not who can be legally compelled to
hand them over under the US CLOUD Act. The same logic applies to picking a
model, it just changes what you're choosing between.

## Which models are genuinely EU-owned?

Two real answers, and one honest asterisk. Mistral AI is French, privately
held, and runs its own European infrastructure end to end, which is the
cleanest "no CLOUD Act exposure" answer on the market today.

Aleph Alpha is the other one, and it comes with a caveat worth stating
plainly. Its enterprise platform, PhariaAI, runs on Schwarz Group's STACKIT
cloud in Germany with no US hyperscaler at any layer, which is a genuinely
sovereign infrastructure stack
([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/cohere-acquires-aleph-alpha-a-deal-born-of-sovereignty-necessity/)).
But Aleph Alpha merged with Canada's Cohere in April 2026. The infrastructure
is still German; the parent company is no longer purely European. If a
client's requirement is "no non-EU parent, full stop," check the current
ownership yourself rather than trusting last year's press release. Marketing
language ages faster than corporate structures change, and sometimes it's
the other way round.

## Can you keep Claude, GPT or Gemini inside the EU?

Yes, for residency. No, for sovereignty. And it depends on which model
version, so check before you build.

Claude runs in-region through AWS Bedrock in Frankfurt, Dublin, Paris and
Stockholm, and through Google Vertex AI's EU regions, but Anthropic's own
direct API defaults to US hosting, and Claude on Microsoft Foundry still has
no EU data zone as of late August 2026 despite being listed as "coming"
([Requesty's regional breakdown](https://www.requesty.ai/eu/claude)).
OpenAI now offers data residency in Europe for the API and for ChatGPT
Enterprise through `eu.api.openai.com`, alongside the UK, US, Japan, Canada
and several other regions
([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-data-residency-in-europe/)).
Google's Gemini gets regional pinning through Vertex AI across Belgium,
the Netherlands, Finland and several other EU locations, but that guarantee
does not automatically carry over to every new model release the day it
ships
([Google Cloud's own data residency docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/data-residency)).

The pattern across all three: the platform having an EU region is not the
same as the specific model you're calling being available in it. Check the
model's own documentation page, not the platform's homepage.

| Provider | Model owner is EU-based | EU-region hosting exists | Signed the GPAI Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mistral AI | Yes | Native | Yes |
| Aleph Alpha (PhariaAI) | German infra, Canadian parent since Apr 2026 | Native | Yes |
| Anthropic Claude | No | Bedrock, Vertex AI (not Foundry) | Yes |
| OpenAI GPT | No | `eu.api.openai.com`, ChatGPT Enterprise | Yes |
| Google Gemini | No | Vertex AI, per model version | Yes |

## Does the Code of Practice actually matter to you as a buyer?

More than it did in August. Around 190 organisations had signed by the end
of July, including Amazon, Anthropic, Google, IBM, Microsoft, Mistral AI and
OpenAI
([General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-Purpose_AI_Code_of_Practice)).
Meta had not signed as of the most recent public count, and neither had the
major Chinese labs, Alibaba, Baidu or DeepSeek. That doesn't make an
unsigned model unusable. It does mean you're relying on your own contract
and DPA to cover ground the Code would otherwise cover for you, which is a
fair trade-off for some use cases and a real gap for others, especially once
a model touches anything a regulator would call high-risk later.

## So what should you actually pick?

Three honest defaults, by situation. No specific compliance requirement and
cost or capability drives the decision: pick whichever model does the job
best, EU-owned or not, and don't pay a sovereignty premium you don't need.
You need EU data residency because a client contract or a DPIA calls for it,
but not full sovereignty: any of Claude, GPT or Gemini through their EU
regions is a defensible, normal choice, same logic as the F2-versus-F4
capacity decision I wrote up for Fabric. You are in a genuinely sensitive
sector, health data, biometric data, public-sector procurement that
specifically requires an EU-only legal chain: Mistral is the cleanest
answer today, and Aleph Alpha is a strong second if the Cohere ownership
question doesn't disqualify it for your case.
