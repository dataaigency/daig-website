Before a surf session I look at the tide table. Not the forecast, the table.
It is a boring grid of times and heights, and it has never once argued with
anyone. The harbour master reads the same grid. So does the skipper, the
fisherman and the kid with the bodyboard. Nobody asks which version is
current, or whether the number came from the sales team or from finance.

Most companies would pay a lot for that kind of agreement about their own
numbers. The tide table has had it for generations.

## What is a tide table, really?

A published prediction of high and low water, for named places, for every day
of a year, calculated well before the year starts. National hydrographic
offices publish them annually. In Germany, for example, the BSH issues tide
tables for the North Sea, the British Isles and the Atlantic coast of Europe
every year
([BSH tide publications](https://eu.nvcharts.com/41714/gezeitentafeln-2025-europaeische-gewaesser/)).

That is already the shape of a good data product: a named owner, a fixed
format, a regular release, and consumers who simply read it.

## How can anyone know the tide a year ahead?

Because the inputs are stable. The standard method is harmonic analysis,
recognised by the International Hydrographic Organization and used by
national offices including SHOM in France, the UKHO and the BSH. It splits
the tide into individual cycles driven by the Earth, Moon and Sun, fits them
to a long record from a tide gauge, and then adds them back up for any future
date, including a correction for the 18.6-year lunar nodal cycle
([Copernicus Ocean Science](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/15/1363/2019/)).

Two details matter for us. The hard work is done once, from years of
observations, and then reused. And the output is a table, not a model you
have to run. The reader never touches the maths.

## What does a tide table not tell you?

Honestly, quite a lot, and the honesty is the point. It predicts the
astronomical tide only. It does not include wind, air pressure or river flow,
which can matter a great deal
([NOAA via Ocean Motion](https://www.oceanmotion.org/html/background/tides-observing.htm)).
Roughly one centimetre of water level per hectopascal of pressure, as a rule
of thumb, so a deep low can lift the sea noticeably above the table.

And the table is not embarrassed by that. Storm surge is simply the
difference between the observed water level and the predicted one. The
prediction stays fixed, so the gap can be measured. A separate warning
service, not the table, deals with storms. In the Netherlands that is KNMI
working with Rijkswaterstaat
([ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2008/17518-probability-forecasts-water-levels-coast-netherlands.pdf)).

Tide tables don't promise everything. They promise one clearly scoped thing,
and they keep that promise.

## What is the business version of a tide table?

Reference data and the definitions built on it. Reference data is the slow,
shared stuff everything else leans on: what counts as an active customer,
what "revenue" includes, which currency rate applies on which date, how
regions roll up. A data contract is the agreement about its shape, owner and
change rules. I wrote about that in [why a band can improvise and your company
cannot](/work/2026-09-band-improvise-governance).

Now picture "revenue". Without a published definition, sales computes it one
way, finance another, and an AI agent asked to summarise the quarter invents
a third from whatever columns it finds. Each is defensible. None matches.
This is the quiet failure behind [folders instead of contracts in the
medallion layers](/work/2026-08-medallion-mistakes).

## How do you publish a KPI like a tide table?

Five habits, borrowed straight from the table.

1. **One owner.** A named person or team, not "the data team".
2. **One scoped promise.** Write down what the number includes and, just as
   plainly, what it leaves out, the way the table leaves out the weather.
3. **An edition.** Put a version and a date on the definition. When it
   changes, publish a new edition and say so, rather than quietly editing.
4. **Compute once, read many.** Build the number in one place, in your gold
   layer, and let dashboards and agents read it instead of recomputing it.
5. **Measure the gap.** Compare observed against expected, the way surge is
   observed minus predicted, and treat a growing gap as a signal.

None of this needs a platform rebuild. A one-page definition and a single
governed table is often enough to start.

## Where does the metaphor stop working?

Three places, and they matter.

First, the tide is physics. Nobody votes on the Moon's orbit. A KPI is a
human decision, so someone has to make that decision and defend it, and the
table's calm authority is partly borrowed from the sky. Second, a tide table
changes slowly and on a calendar. Your business changes mid-year: new
products, new pricing, a merger. Your editions will come faster, and they
need a change process the table never required. Third, the tide has no
adversaries and no incentives. Inside a company, a number can move someone's
bonus, which is exactly why it needs an owner who is not the person being
measured.
