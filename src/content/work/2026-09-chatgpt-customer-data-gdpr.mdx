Somewhere in your company, someone pasted a customer email into ChatGPT this
week to get a cleaner reply. Probably more than one person. The question is
not whether it happens. It is which account they were logged into when it did,
because the answer to "is that GDPR-compliant?" changes completely with that
one detail.

This is a plain-words guide, not legal advice. Your data protection officer or
counsel has the last word. But the practical answer is more settled than the
compliance-vendor blogs make it sound.

## So, can we use ChatGPT with customer data?

Yes, through a business door with a signed data processing agreement (DPA).
No, through a personal account.

The reason is GDPR Article 28. When you send customer personal data to a
vendor to process on your behalf, you stay the controller and the vendor
becomes your processor, and you need a written contract that says so. That is
the same rule that applies to your cloud host or your email tool. If the
vendor is a processor, the customer data stays under your instructions. If
there is no contract, there is no processor relationship, and you have simply
handed personal data to a third party.

OpenAI offers a DPA for its business products and the API, not for consumer
accounts
([iubenda's GDPR review](https://www.iubenda.com/en/blog/openai-gdpr-compliance/),
[OpenAI's DPA](https://openai.com/policies/data-processing-addendum/)).
For customers in the EEA, the contracting party is OpenAI Ireland Limited, and
anything it transfers outside the EEA is covered by standard contractual
clauses or an adequacy decision, as the DPA states.

## What is the difference between the personal, Business and API doors?

Mostly three things: the contract, the training default and where the data sits.

**Personal accounts (Free, Plus, Pro).** No DPA. Conversations are used to
improve the models unless the user switches that off in settings, and the
switch only applies to future chats. Deleted chats can linger on OpenAI's
systems for up to 30 days
([secureprivacy.ai](https://www.secureprivacy.ai/blog/gpt-5-training-data-opt-out)).
This is the door your team is most likely using by accident.

**ChatGPT Business and Enterprise.** A DPA is available, and conversations are
excluded from training by default
([eesel.ai's pricing and plan summary](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-work-pricing)).
This is the sensible default for a team that wants a chat interface. One
catch worth knowing: European data residency covers Enterprise and Edu
workspaces, not Business
([OpenAI's residency announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-data-residency-in-europe/)).

**The API.** A DPA is available, and API data is not used for training unless
you opt in. For products you build yourself, this is the cleanest door,
because you control exactly what gets sent.

I am leaving list prices out on purpose. Every source I found quotes USD only,
and the plans change often. Check the current EUR price on OpenAI's own
pricing page before you budget.

## What does "European data residency" actually cover?

Where your content is stored at rest, and, for some plans, where it is
processed. Not a promise that no non-EU company is involved.

OpenAI announced European data residency for ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Edu
and the API Platform. On the API side, a new Project set to the Europe region
is handled in-region with zero data retention, meaning requests and responses
are not stored at rest. It only works for new Projects, so an existing one
cannot be switched over. For ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu, customer content
(prompts, uploaded files, images) can be stored at rest in Europe, and since
16 January 2026 eligible customers can also choose in-region GPU inference
([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-data-residency-in-europe/),
summarised by [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/openai-introduces-european-data-residency-for-enterprises-and-developers/)).

That is useful. It is also residency, not sovereignty: the provider is still a
US company, which matters for some buyers and not for others. I wrote the long
version in
[An EU region is not sovereignty](/work/2026-09-eu-region-not-sovereignty).

## Do we need a DPIA before we start?

Often yes, when the use is more than "polish this sentence".

A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) under Article 35 is required when
processing is likely to be high risk. The European Data Protection Board's
guidance lists nine criteria, including large-scale processing, sensitive data,
new technology, profiling or scoring, and vulnerable people, and two or more
generally means you need one
([EDPB high-risk criteria, as summarised by Presencis](https://presencis.com/questions/dpia-for-ai-systems/)).
Generative AI counts as new technology on its own, so it takes only one more.

Two examples. Drafting replies to customer emails with names in them is
probably low risk, and a short written assessment is enough. Feeding support
tickets that contain health details, or CVs, into a model to rank people is
squarely DPIA territory. When in doubt, write the assessment. It is cheaper
than explaining its absence.

## Has any regulator actually fined anyone over ChatGPT?

One did, and then a court took it back. The lesson is not what most people draw.

In December 2024 Italy's regulator, the Garante, fined OpenAI €15 million,
citing a missing legal basis for training, weak transparency and an unreported
breach. On 18 March 2026 the Court of Rome annulled that fine, and it did so
on jurisdiction, not on the merits: once OpenAI set up in Ireland, the Irish
Data Protection Commission became its lead authority
([ppc.land's report on the ruling](https://ppc.land/italian-court-kills-openais-eur15m-fine-and-it-wasnt-even-close/),
[ai-regulation.com](https://ai-regulation.com/court-of-rome-openai-gdpr-ruling/)).
The court never ruled the practices lawful. So "the fine was cancelled" is not
a permission slip, and your obligations as a controller did not move an inch.

## What should we do this week?

Five checks, in this order. Print them if you like.

1. **Find the doors already open.** Ask, without blame, who uses AI tools for
   work and on which account.
2. **Close the personal door for customer data.** One clear sentence in your
   policy: no customer personal data in personal accounts, ever.
3. **Pick one business door** and sign its DPA. Business for a chat interface,
   API for something you build.
4. **Match the door to the data.** Sensitive data or a hard residency
   requirement points at Enterprise or an in-region API Project.
5. **Write the DPIA, or write down why you do not need one.** One page is a
   fine start.
