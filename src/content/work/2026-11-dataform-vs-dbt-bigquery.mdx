When I last checked what Google suggests for "dataform vs dbt", one of the
suggestions was "dataform vs dbt reddit". That tells you what people think
of the comparison pages: rows of ticks, a sponsored conclusion, and no
answer for a team of three who just want their tables built every morning.

I build transformations with dbt by default, on BigQuery and on Fabric. So
read this as the view of someone with a habit, who still points small
BigQuery teams at Dataform fairly often.

## Which one should a small BigQuery team pick?

If BigQuery is your only warehouse and you plan to keep it that way, start
with Dataform. If a second warehouse is on the horizon, or you need to hire
people who already know the tool, pick dbt Core. Pay for the hosted dbt
platform only when you would rather rent the scheduler than run it.

The reason this is a calmer decision than the internet suggests: both tools
do the same job. You write SELECT statements in files, point them at each
other with a `ref()`, and the tool works out the build order, creates the
tables and runs your checks. The SQL that lands in BigQuery is yours either
way.

## What does each one actually cost, in euros?

Dataform costs nothing, dbt Core costs nothing, and the hosted dbt platform
costs about €91 per developer a month on its Starter plan. In all three
cases you pay BigQuery separately for the queries.

**Dataform.** Google's
[pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/dataform/pricing) says it in one
line: "Dataform is a free service." You are charged through BigQuery for the
queries it runs, plus whatever you use around it, such as Cloud Logging or a
scheduler. For a small team that surrounding cost is noise next to the query
bill.

**dbt Core.** The open source engine is free under Apache 2.0, and dbt Labs
[says it stays that way](https://www.getdbt.com/blog/fivetran-dbt-20-future)
in the Rust-based v2 that started rolling out in June 2026. Free does not
mean nothing to run, though. Something has to execute `dbt build` on a
schedule: a CI job, a small container, an Airflow task. That runner, its
service account key and its upgrades are yours.

**The dbt platform** (what used to be called dbt Cloud). The
[Developer plan](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/platform/billing/plans-and-billing)
is free for one seat and 3,000 successfully built models a month; go over
and the remaining runs are cancelled until the month resets. The
[Starter plan](https://www.getdbt.com/pricing) lists at $100 per user per
month for up to five developers, 15,000 built models a month and one
project. dbt publishes that price in dollars only. At the ECB reference rate
of 17 November 2026 ($1.0989 to the euro, as published by the
[Banque de France](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/statistics/rates-and-prices/exchange-rates-daily-parities-2026-11-17))
that is about €91 a seat, so roughly €273 a month for three developers and
€455 for five.

"Built models" is the number to watch on the hosted plans. Forty models
rebuilt every hour is close to 29,000 builds a month, which is past the
Starter allowance. The same forty models built once a night is about 1,200.

## What do you give up with Dataform?

Portability, mostly, and a slice of the ecosystem.

- **It is BigQuery only.** Dataform is a Google Cloud product for BigQuery.
  If the company later adds Fabric, Snowflake or Postgres, your
  transformation tool does not come along.
- **The package shelf is short.** dbt has years of community packages for
  common checks and source systems. Dataform has a handful, mostly
  community-built, so you write more helpers yourself.
- **Reuse is JavaScript, not Jinja.** Dataform files are SQLX: SQL with a
  config block and JavaScript for anything dynamic. Some teams prefer it.
  It is still a different skill from the Jinja macros most analytics
  engineers have on their CV.
- **Tests are a little more work.** Dataform has assertions for data quality
  and, since Dataform core 3.0.56,
  [unit tests](https://cloud.google.com/dataform/docs/test-data) with mock
  inputs capped at 100 rows each. dbt's one-line YAML tests are quicker to
  write.
- **The hiring pool is smaller.** "Knows dbt" is on far more job adverts and
  far more CVs. If you depend on contractors, that matters.

## What do you give up with dbt?

Either an afternoon a month or a subscription, and a little simplicity.

With dbt Core you own the plumbing. It is not hard, but it is one more
thing that pages someone when a key expires. With the hosted platform you
swap that for a per-seat bill and a second supplier in the chain.

It is also a tool in the middle of a big year. dbt Labs and Fivetran
[completed their merger](https://www.fivetran.com/press/fivetran-dbt-labs-complete-merger-to-create-the-data-infrastructure-for-trusted-ai-agents)
on 1 June 2026, and the engine is being rewritten at the same time. The
open source licence commitment is public. I would still pin your version
and read the upgrade notes before moving a production project to v2.

## Where do your code and run logs live?

With Dataform, inside the Google Cloud project you already have. With dbt
Core, on your own runner. With the hosted dbt platform, at a second vendor,
which means a second data processing agreement to read.

Neither tool copies your tables anywhere; the data stays in BigQuery. What
moves is the code, the credentials that can reach the warehouse, and the run
logs.

A Dataform repository lives in a region you choose. The
[locations list](https://cloud.google.com/dataform/docs/locations) includes
Belgium, the Netherlands, Frankfurt, Paris and Madrid among others, and it
sits under the same Google Cloud contract as your BigQuery data. There is
nothing new to sign.

The hosted dbt platform has Frankfurt regions, but dbt's
[regions page](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/cloud/about-cloud/access-regions-ip-addresses)
lists them under Enterprise plans. If an EU-hosted account matters to you,
ask where a Starter account will live before you put a warehouse credential
in it. With dbt Core the question does not come up.

The platform choice itself comes first; I compared the two I work on in
[Fabric or BigQuery for a small European team](/work/2026-09-fabric-vs-bigquery).

## Can you switch later?

Yes, and more cheaply than most tooling moves. Your models are SELECT
statements with references in both tools: `${ref("orders")}` in Dataform,
`{{ ref('orders') }}` in dbt. That part is close to search and replace.

What does not port is everything around the SQL: macros or JavaScript
helpers, packages, test definitions and the scheduling setup. So pick for
the team you have this year. The layering matters more than the tool
anyway; the [five medallion mistakes](/work/2026-08-medallion-mistakes)
happen just as easily in either one.

## The comparison, in one table

| | Dataform | dbt Core | dbt platform (Starter) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licence cost | €0 | €0 | about €91 per developer a month ($100) |
| Who runs it | Google, inside your project | You, on a runner you maintain | The vendor |
| Warehouses | BigQuery only | BigQuery, Fabric, Snowflake and more | Same as dbt Core |
| Plan limits | Google Cloud quotas | None | 5 developers, 15,000 built models a month, 1 project |
| Reuse language | JavaScript in SQLX | Jinja macros and packages | Jinja macros and packages |
| Code and logs | Your Google Cloud region | Your own runner | Second vendor; region choice on Enterprise plans |
| Best fit | BigQuery-only team that wants nothing to run | Team that wants portability and hireable skills | Team that wants dbt without running it |

## So which one, honestly?

For a team of one to five people on BigQuery alone, Dataform is the right
first answer more often than dbt people like to admit. It is already in the
console, it costs nothing, and there is nothing to keep alive.

Choose dbt when one of three things is true: you run or expect a second
platform, you hire for the skill, or you lean on packages you do not want
to rewrite. Start with Core. Move to the hosted platform on the day the
runner becomes somebody's least favourite chore.
