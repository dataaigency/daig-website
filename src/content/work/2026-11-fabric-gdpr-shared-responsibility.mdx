"Is Fabric GDPR compliant?" is the question I hear most, and it is slightly
the wrong question. A platform cannot be compliant on your behalf. GDPR
attaches to what you do with personal data, and Fabric is the place you do it.

The better question is a split one. What does Microsoft cover, what does it
leave with you, and where does the line sit? Here is my working answer.

## What does Microsoft actually cover?

The platform side. Microsoft publishes SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 reports for
Fabric ([Fabric blog](https://blog.fabric.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/author/Rick%20Xu)),
and it has announced ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018 and 27701 certificates
([Fabric community update](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/t5/Fabric-Updates-Blog/Updated-Microsoft-Fabric-compliance-offerings/ba-p/5172035)).
Those are independent audits of how the service is run: access control,
change management, incident handling. Useful, and worth attaching to your
vendor file.

It also covers where capacity runs. You pick a region when you create a
capacity, and the workspaces under it keep their content there. The region
stays fixed after creation
([Microsoft's multi-geo docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/admin/service-admin-premium-multi-geo)).
I went through what residency does and does not buy you in
[the sovereignty post](/work/2026-09-eu-region-not-sovereignty), so I will not
repeat it here.

## What do the certificates not tell you?

Three things, and they are the ones auditors and customers actually ask about.

A certificate is not a statement about your use. SOC 2 tells you Microsoft
runs the service in a controlled way. It says nothing about whether you had
a lawful basis to load your customer table into it.

A certificate is not a map of your data. Even with a chosen region, the
multi-geo docs say some tenant metadata stays in the tenant's home region,
and detailed semantic model metadata from scanning is always stored there.
That is usually fine, but "all in the EU" is a sentence to confirm against
the page, not assume. Check your home region in the About dialog of the
Fabric portal
([how to find it](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/admin/find-fabric-home-region)).

And moving later is not instant. When you move content between capacities in
different regions, the source data may remain in the old region for up to 30
days (same multi-geo page). Pick the region once and carefully.

## What is still your job?

Everything about the data itself. In my projects it comes down to three
questions.

**What did you load, and why?** Under GDPR you need a lawful basis and a
purpose for each use of personal data. A lakehouse invites "load everything,
decide later". That is the habit to break. Load what a named report needs,
write down why, and keep that note with the pipeline. It also makes the
record of processing activities nearly free to produce.

**Who can see it?** Workspace roles, row-level security and sensitivity
labels are your configuration, not Microsoft's. The most common finding in a
first look at a stack is a workspace everyone in the company can open, with
a raw customer table in it. The certificate has nothing to say about that.

**Can you actually erase it?** This is the one that surprises people. Fabric
tables are stored as Delta tables, and a Delta table keeps older versions of
its files so you can time travel. A plain DELETE removes the rows from the
current version. The old files stay until a vacuum removes them
([the Delta Lake mechanics, from Databricks' docs](https://docs.databricks.com/gcp/security/privacy/gdpr-delta)).
So an erasure request is a two step job: delete, then vacuum within a period
you can defend, and handle backups and downstream copies on purpose.
Shortening retention to make erasure real also removes your ability to
restore, which is a business decision to take with whoever owns the risk. I
could not find a Microsoft page that walks through this for Fabric
specifically, so test it on your own tables before you promise a deadline to
a data subject.

## What about Copilot and the AI features?

Treat them as a separate switch. Microsoft's documentation describes tenant
settings that control whether data sent to Azure OpenAI may be processed
outside your capacity's region, and Microsoft's blog says capacities inside
the EU data boundary do not need to enable that cross-geo setting, so EU
capacity data is not stored or processed outside the boundary
([Fabric data agent settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/Fabric/data-science/data-agent-tenant-settings)).
Read your tenant's own settings, because wording and defaults change. If a
feature is on and nobody remembers switching it on, that is the finding.

## A checklist you can use this week

1. Write down your tenant home region and each capacity region.
2. Collect Microsoft's data processing terms and audit reports in one folder.
3. List the personal data in Fabric, with a purpose beside each table.
4. Review who can open each workspace. Remove "everyone".
5. Run a test erasure on a copy of one table: delete, vacuum, confirm.
6. Check the Copilot and cross-geo tenant settings and note the result.
