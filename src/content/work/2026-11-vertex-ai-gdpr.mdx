Vertex AI can be used in a GDPR-compliant way. It is not compliant by
default. The agreement is the easy part. What decides it is three settings
that are yours to get right: which endpoint your prompts hit, what gets
retained, and whether you touch Search grounding.

A note before we start. Google has been renaming this product line, and the
documentation now appears under "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform" as well as
Vertex AI. I use the old name because it is what people still search for.

## What does Google actually give you on the paper side?

The contract piece is solid. Under the Google Cloud terms, the Cloud Data
Processing Addendum applies, which makes Google your processor and you the
controller. That is the Article 28 relationship GDPR asks for
([Google Cloud terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/data-processing-addendum)).

Google also states that it does not use your data to train its models
without your permission or instruction, and says so in its Training
Restriction terms
([Google Cloud generative AI privacy whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/genai_privacy_google_cloud.pdf)).

That is the same shape of answer as for the other big vendors: the paper is
fine, and the configuration is where it goes wrong.

## Does an EU region keep my prompts in the EU?

Only if you pick an EU endpoint. Google's own wording is that requests sent
to a global endpoint may be processed in any Google Cloud location around the
world. The EU multi-region endpoint keeps machine learning processing in EU
member states instead, and that area does not include the UK
([Vertex AI data governance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/data-governance)).

So the first gate is boring and easy to miss. A team copies a code sample,
the sample points at a global endpoint, and the prompt with a customer's name
in it is processed somewhere you never chose. Nothing is broken. Nothing
warns you. The fix is a line of config, and a check in code review.

Residency is also not sovereignty. A US company can still be compelled by US
law whatever the server location. I covered that distinction in
[An EU region is not sovereignty](/work/2026-09-eu-region-not-sovereignty),
and it applies here as it does everywhere.

## Does Vertex AI keep my prompts?

By default, some data is kept for a limited time, and zero data retention is
something you set up, not something you get. Google's documentation says that
to reach zero data retention you must disable data caching. It also says
that if you are in scope for prompt logging for abuse monitoring, you can
request an exception
([Vertex AI data governance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/data-governance)).

Read the scope part twice. Per that page, prompt logging for abuse
monitoring applies to customers whose use of Google Cloud runs under the
standard online terms and who do not have an invoiced billing account. If you
buy through a reseller or on invoice terms, your situation can differ. This
is a question for your Google contract, not for a blog post, and I would not
guess.

The practical lesson: "we are on Vertex, so nothing is stored" is not a
sentence you can put in a DPIA. "We disabled caching and requested the abuse
monitoring exception, and here is the date" is.

## What about grounding with Google Search?

This one is the sharp edge. Google's documentation says there is no way to
disable storage of the related data when you use Grounding with Google
Search, and it points to Web Grounding for Enterprise as the option for zero
retention
([Vertex AI data governance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/data-governance)).

Some third-party pages put a number on how long that data is kept. I could
not match any of them to a Google page, so I am not quoting one. Look it up
at the source when you write your records.

The risk is a feature switch that undoes your careful setup. You turn on
grounding to make answers fresher. A prompt containing personal data now
leaves a trail you thought you had closed.

## So what should you do on Monday?

Six steps, in the order I would do them.

1. Confirm the Cloud Data Processing Addendum is in your agreement, and
   write down which entity signed.
2. Point every call at an EU endpoint, and add a test that fails if a
   global endpoint appears in config.
3. Disable caching for workloads with personal data.
4. Ask whether you are in scope for abuse monitoring logging, and if yes,
   request the exception. Keep the confirmation.
5. Keep Search grounding away from prompts with personal data, or use the
   enterprise variant.
6. Decide whether the use needs a DPIA. If prompts carry customer data at
   scale, assume yes, and write the settings above into it.

None of this is exotic. It is the same discipline as pinning a BigQuery
dataset to a location. Choose once, on purpose, and record it.
