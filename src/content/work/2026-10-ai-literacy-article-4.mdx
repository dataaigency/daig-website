If your company uses ChatGPT, Copilot, or any AI feature inside a tool you
already pay for, Article 4 of the EU AI Act probably applies to you. It is the
quietest obligation in the Act, and since 2 August 2026 it is the one national
authorities can actually enforce. It also just got softer. Softer is not the
same as gone.

## What does Article 4 of the AI Act say now?

Providers and deployers of AI systems must take measures to support the
development of AI literacy among their staff and the other people who run or
use AI systems on their behalf. The earlier wording asked you to ensure, "to
their best extent", a sufficient level of literacy. The Digital Omnibus
(Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026) dropped the
"sufficient level" and added that you do not have to guarantee any specific
level of literacy for any person
([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published),
[Lawandtechnology.eu](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/ai-literacy-digital-omnibus-article-4-ai-act/)).
So the duty moved from a result to an effort. You still have to make the effort.

One thing to watch: the Commission's first proposal would have removed the
company duty entirely and left it to the Commission and Member States to
encourage training. That is not what was adopted. Some summaries written
before the final text still describe the proposal, so check the date on
whatever you read. The final text keeps the duty on you, and adds a duty on
the Commission and Member States to support companies, especially small ones.

## Who has to do something, and who checks?

If you use AI systems in a professional setting, you are a "deployer", and that
is most companies with a ChatGPT subscription. The people covered are your
staff and anyone else operating AI on your behalf, so contractors and agency
freelancers count too. Supervision sits with national market surveillance
authorities, not the AI Office, and they have been able to enforce since
2 August 2026
([European Commission AI literacy Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy)).
I could not find a fixed fine written into Article 4 itself, so penalties come
from national rules and will differ by country. Do not read that as "nothing
happens". Read it as "nobody can tell you the price in advance".

## What counts as "AI literacy" for a 30-person company?

Nothing that needs a certificate. The Act asks you to take account of people's
knowledge, experience, education and the context where the AI is used, and
the amended text no longer asks you to guarantee any particular level. The
Commission's Q&A also stresses documenting what you do. Its public repository of practices lists
more than 40 real initiatives, from e-learning to workshops, with a clear
warning: copying one does not give you a presumption of compliance
([AI Office living repository](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/repository-ai-literacy-practices)).

In practice, "enough" for most small teams is three things people can say out
loud: what the tool does and does not do, what must never go into it (customer
data, contracts, anything under NDA), and who to ask when unsure. A person who
drafts marketing copy needs a different conversation than a person who
summarises HR files.

## How do you build the paper trail in one afternoon?

Follow the chain in the diagram. The point is the record at the end, because
"we told people in a meeting once" will not survive a question.

1. **List the tools.** Ask every team what AI they use, including the free
   accounts. The real list is always longer than the approved one.
2. **List the people.** Staff, contractors, freelancers who use those tools for you.
3. **Group by role.** Two or three groups is plenty. Sales, finance and
   whoever builds things.
4. **Pick one measure per group.** A 45-minute session, a one-page rule sheet,
   a short recorded walkthrough. Small and real beats big and imaginary.
5. **Write it down.** Date, who attended or read it, what it covered. One
   spreadsheet is fine. Re-run it when you add a tool or a hire.

## What does this have to do with your data?

Everything, and this is where I spend my days. The question people ask in the
session is almost always "what can I paste in here?" You can only answer that
if you know what data you have and where the sensitive parts live. If the
honest answer is "we are not sure", the literacy session is where that gap
