02 · Color

Navy is the ground. Green is the signal.

Roughly 90% of any screen is navy or paper. Flash green appears where something happens: a CTA, a seam, one accent per viewport. If green is everywhere, it means nothing.

Contrast law: flash green is UI-safe on navy (≈13:1) — buttons, seams, marks. On paper it is decoration-only (fails for text): never set copy in green on light grounds. Body text: ice on navy, graphite on paper. Headings: paper on navy, deep navy on paper.

04 · The stripe system

−45°, three permitted forms. Nothing else.

1 · Loud seam (12px) — announces every navy↔paper transition and the page bottom. repeating-linear-gradient(-45deg, #03F856 0 9px, #061034 9px 22px)

2 · Quiet seam (12px) — transitions between two dark sections. repeating-linear-gradient(-45deg, #16244E 0 9px, #061034 9px 22px)

Headline can sit beside it — never on it

3 · Hero field — ONE per page, hairline stripes at ≤14% opacity, off to a side. rgba(3,248,86,0.13) 0 3px, transparent 3px 26px + skewX(-8deg)

DO: seams full-bleed; hero field behind empty space; stripe angle always −45°.
DON'T: stripes behind text, inside cards, as borders, at other angles, in other colors, or more than one loud element per viewport.
